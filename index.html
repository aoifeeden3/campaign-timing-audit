A third of all outreach happened in the worst-converting month.

An audit of 4,521 real direct-marketing phone contacts from a Portuguese bank's term-deposit campaign (Moro et al., 2014) finds that 30.9% of all calls were placed in May — the single lowest-converting month in the entire campaign. Meanwhile October, the best-converting month, received under 2% of total call volume.

Why this likely happened

Campaigns like this typically run on a fixed calendar and staffing plan rather than a performance-responsive one — call center capacity was probably scheduled well in advance, and May's volume may simply reflect when staff and lead lists were available, not when leads were warmest. The result is a classic operational-calendar-vs-performance-calendar mismatch.

What the numbers actually show

Autumn months (September, October) and December convert 3–4x higher than the May baseline, on a fraction of the volume. Whatever is different about leads contacted in those windows — seasonality, list quality, or timing relative to financial decisions — it's not being capitalized on at scale.

Reallocation recommendation

Shift a meaningful share of May's call volume into September, October, and December windows, even on the same total lead list — the conversion math favors timing over raw volume by a wide margin here.

Cap outreach at 2–3 contacts per lead; conversion rate declines steadily past the first attempt and bottoms out at 6+ contacts, suggesting diminishing returns rather than persistence paying off.

Prioritize call capacity toward leads with a prior successful campaign outcome — that segment converts at 64%, over 5x the baseline, and is likely under-resourced relative to its value.
